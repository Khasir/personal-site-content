I play a lot of [Chivalry 2](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1824220/Chivalry_2/). I have a love-hate relationship with it, as I slice hundreds of heads off with my greatsword late into the night.[^1] It’s a game where you can live out the fantasy of being a medieval knight carving your way through enemy lines. I even used to watch YouTubers like [Ziggylata](https://www.youtube.com/@Ziggylata/featured) and [Soter Dave](https://www.youtube.com/@SoterDave/featured) because I loved the idea of swinging my sword even when I wasn’t playing.

I had the pleasure of being in the same Discord call as Soter Dave once. I don’t remember why exactly, but it must have been a gaming clan event that I was participating in. I stayed muted for the most part, as there were over a dozen other people in the call, and I was more socially anxious then. Someone with a perfectly smooth radio voice was cracking jokes. It took me a moment to realize it was Dave making jokes—I had thought someone was playing a video in the background. He was also cueing sounds from a soundboard with great comedic timing. He was hilarious! But no one else on the call was laughing. I was laughing, muted, so he couldn’t hear me.

Unfortunately, Soter Dave took his own life about a year later. It happened soon after being permanently banned from the game due to a misunderstanding. I still think about what burdens must have led to the final straw breaking him, what demons he must have been facing, what he was hiding from others by being the jovial entertainer. What if someone had told him how funny he was in that call? What if someone had taken the time to simply notice him? *Wow Dave, your videos are great. You’re a funny guy.* Would he have had second thoughts?

I have been fortunate enough to not have had anyone in my close friends or family pass away during my lifetime. But when I was younger, a friend of a friend in elementary school passed away from cancer. I didn’t know him very well, and I’ve forgotten his name, but I remember his face. He was quieter, respectful, friendly—not rowdy like most others his age.

During my masters, I stayed with a senior as part of a homeshare program. She should be past her mid-80s now. I’ve been meaning to visit her sometime. I mail a Christmas card to her every year. She didn’t reply last year.

More recently, a trailblazer of the Canadian civic tech community passed away suddenly a few weeks ago. Curtis McCord completed his PhD by studying civic tech in the Canadian context, with two of the three main parts of [his thesis](https://www.proquest.com/openview/a25597d67991596d22f2a4e01d326408/1?pq-origsite=gscholar&cbl=18750&diss=y) focusing on Civic Tech Toronto. I’ve run into him a few times. Last time I saw Curtis was at [Democracy XChange](https://www.democracyxchange.org/). I’ll see him again next time.

Funny how you don’t think about how anything you do could be for the last time until it hits you.

Funny how we feel the impacts of our predecessors even and *especially* once they’re gone.

Funny how I recently started [my own project](/interviews) to document civic tech in Toronto.

Funny how the timing works out.

Funny thing, life.

In February, I went to a concert by <span style="font-size: 11px;">(</span><span style="font-size: 15px;">(</span>( O )<span style="font-size: 15px;">)</span><span style="font-size: 11px;">)</span>, the self-proclaimed unpronounceable force who releases sundrops every year.[^1] By 2032, she plans to have published her complete collection of 12 sundrops. Her sundrops usually come out on August 27, her birthday, but the next one has been [delayed](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dccs8ftK6ge/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&igsi=MzRlODBiNWFlZA==)—just like this blog post, which I originally planned to release ~~a few months ago~~ a few days ago.

At the concert, I was reflecting on how a popular artist like her had opted for such a small venue, instead of a larger one that I’m sure she could have packed with adoring fans. Regardless, she soon began, sitting cross-legged on stage and dancing to the music. I could hardly see her, but neither could most of the audience beyond the front row. I don’t think she minded.

{% include figure.html src="/content/images/june-in-feb-2026.jpeg" alt="((( O ))) performing at the Garrison in Toronto." caption="((( O )))" %}

I discovered June on YouTube around a decade ago through another artist, FKJ, her now husband. They were improvising a beat, synced to each other’s rhythms—just [vibing out](https://youtu.be/9Gq9N-sPdYg?si=y6OHllPVjQjeGvua). The original video’s since been taken down by her publisher, but there’s still a [ripped version](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yz9q-iO4O40) out there that captures the moment. 

Listening to it brings me back to when I’d study with my siblings all together in our dining-turned-study room. I used to play my music out loud while we were hanging out. That’s something that we’ve carried forward to this day whenever the three of us are together, playing music. I used to pick most of the songs back then, but they’ve since diversified their own music tastes. My brother likes Japanese music, while my sister listens to lots of K-pop.[^2] I listen to a lot of EDM.[^3]

I had a great time at the concert. June is a great singer, dancer, songwriter, you name it. Great sense of humour too—one of my favourite moments was when she and her backup dancers performed her song about a [cat](https://youtu.be/FIazfRHzcRY?si=9OuASA2sp1t0kvRy).

At one point, an audience member shouted, “louder!” Her voice was being drowned out by the instruments. She paused her performance, and quietly replied that she mixed it that way on purpose. But at the encouragement of the crowd, she got the sound technician to turn up her mic. 

You’d never get this kind of interaction at a Coldplay concert.

> Working from a hand-built treehouse studio, using a mixture of traditional and modern instruments, <span style="font-size: 11px;">(</span><span style="font-size: 15px;">(</span>( O )<span style="font-size: 15px;">)</span><span style="font-size: 11px;">)</span> is totally reliant on solar power to pursue her deeply spiritual strain of electronic soul, a limitation that she finds inspiring rather than confining. “*If there is sun then I have the gift to create,*” she smiles. “*But if I don’t have any sun that day, then it’s not meant to be.*”
> 
> — [Interview with Gem Samways, Berlin in Stereo](https://web.archive.org/web/20220122115331/http://berlininstereo.com/o-interview/)
> {: .attribution}

I had gone alone, but I ended up chatting with a fellow Londoner. Soon her friends showed up and we were all enjoying the music together. I got to know them a bit afterwards and they invited me to visit them in London. I have yet to visit, but it’s nice to meet such kind-hearted and open people in the wild.

Sometimes I think about the little things that have shaped me into the person I am. The slow accumulation of everyday interactions and chance encounters. One-off comments and moments of [dès vu](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bPKoIZn6IJI). Going small and going [home](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kTTBpbMMDDc&list=PLE9qm2Qm2YSQ&pp=sAgC).

If home is where the heart is, that’s where I want to be.
